01ICP Deep Dive

The brief's "$500K–20M GMV" ICP is one line covering two buyers.

A 5-person solo-operator brand and an 80-person org with a dedicated growth function don't buy the same way. Splitting the range in two and running each half through the same funnel: Firmographic → Demographic → Pain Points (5-Why) → Root Cause → Insight surfaces two different blockers, not one bigger one.

Root cause chain

Headline pain: rising CAC, no channel diversification

Why 1

Meta/Google costs keep climbing, no alternative acquisition channel.

Why 2

Never tried affiliate/influencer beyond occasional unpaid DMs to creators.

Why 3

Assumes affiliate programs need dedicated headcount or agency budget they don't have.

Why 4

Every case study they've seen is from a brand that "made it," with implied resources they lack.

Why 5 - Root cause

They've never seen a resource-constrained, mid-market-sized brand run this successfully. No reference point that says "this is doable at my size." Not a resource gap, but a proof gap.

Category-stage mismatch Time scarcity
Core insight

They're not under-informed about affiliate marketing. They're under-convinced it's for someone at their size, right now. Positioning has to close that gap before any feature conversation, and hold it across the whole save-and-revisit cycle.

Segment B: Established

Root cause chain

Headline pain: can't prove affiliate revenue is incremental

Why 1

No clean way to separate affiliate-driven sales from sales that would've happened anyway.

Why 2

Attribution model treats last-click/coupon-code conversions as 100% affiliate-driven, no holdout comparison.

Why 3

Tooling was built for operational tracking (who gets paid what), not incrementality testing.

Why 4

The Growth Manager must defend channel ROI to a CFO comparing it against Meta/Google's mature attribution.

Why 5 - Root cause

The category's tooling optimizes for operational tracking, not investment-grade reporting. A reporting-maturity gap, not a performance gap.

Operating-model gap Switching inertia
Core insight

This buyer doesn't need convincing that affiliate marketing works. They need defensibility and operational infrastructure to keep proving it, month over month, to someone above them. The sale isn't the program; it's the reporting and recruitment engine behind it.

03Growth Motion & Channel Strategy

Product-Led Growth, with one conditional exit ramp.

Product-Led Growth is the default journey for all mid-market merchants. Every merchant starts with a self-serve trial and in-product onboarding. Only high-intent Segment B accounts that meet predefined Product Qualified Lead (PQL) criteria receive additional sales-touched support from existing customer support. The buying journey is the same for both segments until the final purchase decision.

Hypothesis

A product-led funnel can carry both segments through Install → Trial → Activation without a segment-specific acquisition motion, because the point where A and B's buying behavior diverges is late at the commit decision, not at discovery or evaluation.

KPI to validate

Trial→Paid CVR uplift for Segment B accounts that receive the assist overlay vs. self-serve baseline - full test design in Section 5.

3.2Why not the alternatives
Sales-Led

Segment B ACV ~$1.8K–3.6K/yr vs. a fully-loaded AE at ~$120K/yr → a rep needs 40–65 closed deals/yr to break even. CAC exceeds LTV at this ACV.

Inbound (content-led)

Segment A's blocker is a proof/trust gap, not an information gap. Kept as a top-of-funnel input (SEO/blog), not the conversion mechanism.

Paid Digital (as primary)

Learning-phase time would consume 30–50%+ of the window before signal is reliable. Kept only as retargeting on the existing 750K traffic pool.

ABM

Personalization cost only pays off at high ACV → doesn't clear that bar. Also assumes a committee; Segment B is a two-person buying unit.

Partnerships

No independent conversion logic: a listing routes the prospect into the same PLG funnel. Treated as a channel extension, not a separate motion.

05Key Risk & How We'd Validate It

Testing the highest-risk assumption

The assumption

A product-led funnel with a light, conditional sales-assist overlay is sufficient to convert Segment B merchants at a defensible rate without a dedicated, rep-driven sales motion.

Why this is the highest-leverage risk

Every other recommendation in this assessment: positioning, channel mix, or success metrics can be refined over time. This assumption cannot. If Segment B requires a dedicated sales motion instead of a product-led approach, the entire go-to-market strategy changes. Validating this early reduces the risk of investing in the wrong operating model.

Test hypothesis · 2–4 weeks, low-cost

Segment B trial accounts that receive a guided-setup + sales-assist touch within 48 hours of signup will convert to paid at a meaningfully higher rate than self-serve-only accounts, at a cost-to-serve that stays viable within existing CS team capacity, no new headcount.

Control: 10–15 merchants
Standard self-serve

PLG funnel, no intervention. Sample: 20–30 trial accounts matching the Segment B firmographic proxy (Shopify Plus flag, or GMV-proxy via order volume/AOV in the $2M–20M range), over a 2–4 week signup window.

Variant: 10–15 merchants
Guided setup + assist

Existing CS team reaches out within 48h with a guided setup call, per the PQL-trigger logic in 3.1. No new hires, no new tooling, capacity carved from current CS bandwidth for the test window only.

Uplift ≥ 1.5× and cost-to-serve within capacity

Validates the hybrid PLG + assist overlay. Formalize the PQL trigger as a permanent, scoped process. No dedicated sales hire needed yet.

Real uplift, but cost-to-serve too high to scale

The assist needs to be lighter-touch (async guided setup, in-app checklist) rather than human-led. Redesign the overlay, not the motion.

No meaningful uplift

Segment B's blocker isn't "needs a human", most likely the incrementality-reporting gap from the ICP research. Redirect the next investment cycle toward self-serve reporting/proof features instead of a sales-assist overlay.
